**Interpretation: literal.** The genre's elements are real and concrete. Its threats, powers, or phenomena (monsters, magic, conspiracies, crimes) actually exist in the story and cause tangible problems. They can echo the characters' emotional conflicts, but they are never only metaphors.
<!-- @@END@@ -->

<!-- @@LEVEL: grounded@@ -->
**Interpretation: grounded.** The genre's elements are present but ambiguous and rooted in realism. Strange events can have natural or human explanations as well as supernatural ones; the evidence leaves room for doubt, and the story need not settle which reading is true.
<!-- @@END@@ -->

<!-- @@LEVEL: mixed@@ -->
**Interpretation: mixed.** The genre's elements work on two levels at once. They are real within the story and cause concrete problems, and each also mirrors a human conflict (a relationship under strain, a social pressure, a private fear), so dealing with the genre threat means dealing with what it reflects.
<!-- @@END@@ -->

<!-- @@LEVEL: symbolic@@ -->
**Interpretation: symbolic.** The genre's conventions are a frame for human conflict. Its "monsters," "magic," or "crimes" are social, emotional, or systemic problems given genre shape and genre names; the story keeps the genre's structure and feel while its stakes stay human-scale.
<!-- @@END@@ -->
