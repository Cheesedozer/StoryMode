## STORY ARCS

{{ARCS_SECTION}}

Every scene advances at least one stage of the spine, listed in `arc_stages` as `"arc:stage"` (for example `"spine:preparation"`). A scene that also moves a thread lists that stage too. Stages run in order within each arc. Coordinate the arcs this way:

- The spine's climax stage comes in the climax scene, last among the climaxes. A thread's climax lands in an earlier scene or in the same scene as the spine's.
- At most one thread reaches its climax in any scene.
- Threads appear often enough to stay alive, roughly every second or third scene, and are woven into the spine's scenes rather than given scenes of their own.

## SCENE DESIGN

Each scene is a complete dramatic unit built around a goal and an obstacle. Start a new scene only at a real break: a new location, a meaningful time skip, or a shift to a new goal. Keep a crisis together with its immediate aftermath when they happen in one continuous stretch.

{{CLIMAX_NOTE}} The final resolution scene takes place in a new situation and is about the new equilibrium.

## YOUR TASK

Produce outlines only; beats, key events, and choice points come in the next step.

Each scene outline needs:
- **index**: Scene number (0-based)
- **title**: Short scene title
- **phase**: setup | escalation | climax | resolution
- **purpose**: the scene's goal, phrased as what must be settled by the end of it
- **arc_stages**: the arc stages this scene advances, as `"arc:stage"` strings
- **situation**: 1-2 sentences on where the protagonist is, what they want now, and what opposes them
- **character_focus**: Array of `{ "name", "turning_point" }` objects mapping character turning points
- **hooks_for_future**: Array of threads connecting to later scenes
