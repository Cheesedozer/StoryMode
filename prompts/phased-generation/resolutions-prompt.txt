## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Design three endings the story can reach, each the result of a different pattern of choices by the protagonist. Together they cover the main ways the central conflict can resolve.

- Each ending has a **reached_if** condition: the tags that lead there. The ending whose condition best matches what the player actually did is the one the story moves toward. Make the conditions distinguish the endings from each other.
- **primary_ending** is the ending that best fits the story's tone and the arcs as planned, if the player follows the most natural path. It is also the fallback when no other ending matches, so its reached_if can be light or empty.
- **alternate_endings** are two meaningfully different outcomes: different winners, costs, or answers to the story's central question, not variations in detail.
- Each ending's description starts with the choices and events that lead there, then describes the climax's outcome and its aftermath.
- Character outcomes follow from each character's arc in Phase 2, whether completed or deliberately subverted. For the player persona, describe their situation at the end rather than their feelings.
- Match the story's tone: a dark story can end darkly, a hopeful one with hope. An ending can leave questions open if its emotional arc is complete.

### alternate_endings (array of exactly 2)

Each has the same shape as primary_ending.

### complications (array of 6-10)

Complications are brought in when the story stalls: when several exchanges pass without progress, the runtime picks the most specific complication whose condition holds. Each one is a pressure the world applies because of what has or has not happened: a consequence of an earlier choice, a rival's move, a deadline arriving. Make most of them depend on tags or stages, and keep two or three general ones with an empty condition.

```json
{
  "id": "snake_case_id",
  "name": "short name",
  "when": { "tags_all": ["used_real_name"] },
  "situation": "1-2 sentences: what happens, as events and other characters' actions",
  "once": true
}
```

### blueprint_title (string)

A specific, evocative title of 2-6 words drawn from this story's own people, places, or central conflict. Write it after the endings.

### cover_prompt (string)

50-100 words for an image generator: setting, key visual elements, mood, composition, and palette that reflect this story's tone and central conflict.

### opening_message (string)

The first message the player sees, 400-600 words, establishing Scene 1:
- Build it from Scene 1's situation in the scene plan, in the story's narrative voice and atmosphere.
- Narrate the world and the other characters. Describe what the player's character sees and is confronted with, and leave their words, actions, and feelings to the player.
- End on something happening (a character addressing the player's character, an arrival, a discovery, a threat), so the player has something concrete to respond to. End in narration rather than with a question.
- Write it as narrative prose only.
