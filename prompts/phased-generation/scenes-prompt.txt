## STORY ARCS AND CHAPTERS

{{ARCS_SECTION}}

Group the scenes into chapters. A chapter is a stretch of the story that moves one or more arc stages forward and holds one to three scenes, usually two. Each chapter lists the stages it advances in `advances` as `"arc:stage"` strings (for example `"spine:preparation"`). Every chapter advances a spine stage; a chapter that also moves a thread lists that stage too. Stages run in order within each arc. Coordinate the arcs this way:

- The spine's climax stage comes in the chapter that holds the climax scene, last among the climaxes. A thread's climax lands in an earlier chapter or in the same chapter as the spine's.
- At most one thread reaches its climax in any chapter.
- Threads appear often enough to stay alive, roughly every second chapter, and are woven into the spine's scenes rather than given scenes of their own.

## TENTPOLES

Tentpoles are 2 to 4 situations that happen whatever the protagonist has done, at a set point in the story. The player steers how the protagonist meets one, and so how it turns out, but cannot stop it arriving, the way an oncoming car arrives whether or not you saw it coming. Write each as events and other characters' actions.

Each tentpole has a trigger: the chapter it arrives in (`{ "chapter": 3 }`, 0-based) or an arc stage (`{ "stage": "spine:execution" }`). Plant each tentpole's setup hooks in earlier scenes so it feels earned. A tentpole is brought in during play when its trigger is reached, so the scenes of its chapter leave room for it instead of scripting it as a beat.

## SCENE DESIGN

Each scene is a complete dramatic unit built around a goal and an obstacle:

- **purpose**: the scene's goal, phrased as what must be settled by the end of it (for instance "win the magistrate's support" or "get out of the city before dawn").
- **chapter**: the index of the chapter this scene belongs to.
- **situation**: 1–2 sentences on where the protagonist is, what they want right now, and what opposes them in this scene.
- **key_events_if_unchallenged**: 3–5 things the opposition and other characters will do in this scene if the protagonist doesn't intervene. The world keeps moving; the player's choices change or stop these events.
- **choice_points**: 0–2 story-altering decisions, with example approaches the player might take, where each would lead, and a short tag for each approach. The tags are how the story remembers the choice: later complications and endings check for them.
- **beats**: 3–7 milestones that mark progress through the scene.
- **character_focus**: required when a scene carries a turning point.
- Optional: **hooks_for_future** (threads paid off in later scenes) and **expected_rounds** (rough number of exchanges the scene should take).

Beat types: **establishment** (set the scene) | **hook** (create interest) | **reaction** (a character responds) | **escalation** (raise the stakes) | **pivot** (change direction) | **emotional** (an emotional moment) | **transition** (bridge to the next scene)

Beats describe what the world and the non-player characters do, reveal, or demand. When a beat involves the player persona, it sets up the situation they face; their action, decision, and emotional reaction are left to the player.

**Scene boundaries.** Start a new scene only at a real break: a new location, a meaningful time skip, or a shift to a new goal. Keep a crisis together with its immediate aftermath and consequences when they happen in one continuous stretch; a bridge collapse and the scramble to escape it are one scene.

**Climax and resolution.** {{CLIMAX_NOTE}} The final resolution scene takes place in a new situation (later, elsewhere, or clearly separated), and is about the new equilibrium rather than the climax's last minutes.
