Every scene advances at least one stage of the spine, listed in `arc_stages` as `"arc:stage"` (for example `"spine:preparation"`). A scene that also moves a thread lists that stage too. Stages run in order within each arc. Coordinate the arcs this way:

- The spine's climax stage comes in the climax scene, last among the climaxes. A thread's climax lands in an earlier scene or in the same scene as the spine's.
- At most one thread reaches its climax in any scene.
- Threads appear often enough to stay alive, roughly every second or third scene, and are woven into the spine's scenes rather than given scenes of their own.

## SCENE DESIGN

Each scene is a complete dramatic unit built around a goal and an obstacle:

- **purpose**: the scene's goal, phrased as what must be settled by the end of it (for instance "win the magistrate's support" or "get out of the city before dawn").
- **arc_stages**: the arc stages this scene advances, as `"arc:stage"` strings.
- **situation**: 1–2 sentences on where the protagonist is, what they want right now, and what opposes them in this scene.
- **key_events_if_unchallenged**: 3–5 things the opposition and other characters will do in this scene if the protagonist doesn't intervene. The world keeps moving; the player's choices change or stop these events.
- **choice_points**: 0–2 story-altering decisions, with example approaches the player might take and where each would lead.
- **beats**: 3–7 milestones that mark progress through the scene.
- **character_focus**: required when a scene carries a turning point.
- Optional: **hooks_for_future** (threads paid off in later scenes) and **expected_rounds** (rough number of exchanges the scene should take).

Beat types: **establishment** (set the scene) | **hook** (create interest) | **reaction** (a character responds) | **escalation** (raise the stakes) | **pivot** (change direction) | **emotional** (an emotional moment) | **transition** (bridge to the next scene)

Beats describe what the world and the non-player characters do, reveal, or demand. When a beat involves the player persona, it sets up the situation they face; their action, decision, and emotional reaction are left to the player.

**Scene boundaries.** Start a new scene only at a real break: a new location, a meaningful time skip, or a shift to a new goal. Keep a crisis together with its immediate aftermath and consequences when they happen in one continuous stretch; a bridge collapse and the scramble to escape it are one scene.

**Climax and resolution.** {{CLIMAX_NOTE}} The final resolution scene takes place in a new situation (later, elsewhere, or clearly separated), and is about the new equilibrium rather than the climax's last minutes.
